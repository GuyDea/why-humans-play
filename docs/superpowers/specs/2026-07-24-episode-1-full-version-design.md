# Episode 1 Eight-Minute Expansion — Design

- **Status:** Accepted for narration prototyping
- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Target:** `Why AI Makes Bad Advice Feel Right`
- **Branch:** `feat/episode-1-full-version`

## Purpose

Expand the approved 784-word Episode 1 narrative core into a natural eight-minute episode
without padding it into a longer catalogue of studies. The full version will use one
recurring hypothetical decision to turn the research into a continuous story and to show
the four-question method producing a visibly better decision process.

The target is approximately 1,350 spoken words. At roughly 165–170 words per minute, that
supports an approximately eight-minute explanatory delivery with room for emphasis and
jokes. The final runtime must be checked with an actual read after Martin reviews the
complete narration.

## Accepted decisions

1. Target approximately eight minutes rather than the three-minute prototype target.
2. Use one recurring decision as the primary expansion method.
3. Keep the existing studies as the evidence and make the recurring decision explicitly
   hypothetical.
4. Preserve the approved thesis, practical test, strongest comic lines, and final lesson.
5. Add length through mechanism, consequence, and application—not extra terminology or
   decorative background.

## Recurring decision contract

The hypothetical begins from the existing prompt:

> My boss keeps ignoring my brilliant ideas. Should I quit?

The first answer should be persuasive enough to demonstrate the danger without becoming a
caricature. It may sensibly discuss leaving, but it must also visibly inherit the prompt's
unsupported cast: the ideas are brilliant, the boss is simply ignoring them, and quitting
is the relevant decision.

The scenario then returns at each causal step:

1. **Framing:** identify the roles and alternatives the prompt supplied or excluded.
2. **Agreement:** show how a preference-compatible answer can feel helpful.
3. **Presentation:** transform the user's hunch into a polished framework without adding
   evidence.
4. **Feedback:** show how the returned confidence can influence the user's next judgment.
5. **Inspection:** run the four questions and surface missing assumptions, the strongest
   counter-case, a plausible failure path, and the deciding facts that require verification.

The method must not force a predetermined recommendation to stay or quit. A successful
inspection produces a more conditional answer and a concrete evidence checklist. The
observable improvement is that the recommendation now changes when a named deciding fact
changes.

## Approved language baseline

Preserve these lines unless a local transition requires Martin-approved revision:

- “The machine wasn't giving a second opinion. It was giving the first opinion a software
  update.”
- “AI receives one side's court filing—and produces a verdict in bullet points.”
- “Say ‘Absolutely’ first and search for reality during the follow-up.”
- “‘Helpful assistant’ had briefly been promoted to ‘professional accomplice.’”
- “Your hunch enters the chat wearing sweatpants.”
- “It just received headings and institutional lighting.”
- The definition of the borrowed-authority loop.
- “You photocopied the first ballot and counted it twice.”
- All four Second-Opinion Test questions.
- “It is still one system changing chairs.”
- “Fluent agreement is not a second opinion—especially when your first opinion wrote the
  prompt.”

The opening promise remains literal and joke-free. New humor should expose the mechanism,
not interrupt the explanation or mock the person making the decision.

## Evidence and factual boundaries

- Reuse the eight existing evidence records for the current factual claims.
- Do not add a new named study, date, institution, quotation, or real-world incident during
  narration prototyping without a later evidence record.
- The quitting scenario and any sample AI wording must be labeled hypothetical and must not
  be represented as output from a real product or study.
- The borrowed-authority loop and Second-Opinion Test remain original editorial synthesis,
  not established scientific terms or validated diagnostic instruments.
- The cited studies establish their bounded findings separately. Their combination in
  personal AI advice remains an evidence-bounded editorial inference.
- AI-generated opposition supplies useful friction, not independent verification.

## Artifact flow

1. Preserve the current evidence-backed production document while creative expansion is
   underway, except for reconciling its approved runtime metadata.
2. Draft the complete narration at
   `whp-youtube/drafts/01-why-ai-makes-bad-advice-feel-right-full-prototype.md`.
3. Show Martin the whole narration before running timing, retention, evidence, or production
   audits.
4. Refine only the passages Martin targets and obtain explicit creative approval of the
   complete narration.
5. Promote the approved narration into
   `whp-youtube/episodes/01-why-ai-makes-bad-advice-feel-right.md`.
6. Update its word count, beat timings, claims, visuals, application block, issue ledger,
   and evidence records; then run the separate audits and structural validator.

This separation prevents an unfinished prototype from leaving the annotated production
appendix apparently current when it is not.

## Success criteria

- The complete narration lands near 1,350 words before the timing audit.
- The opening question, real study, viewer relevance, and by-end promise all remain clear
  within the opening beat.
- Every abstract mechanism is followed by the recurring scenario, a documented experiment,
  or a concrete consequence.
- Each return to the quitting scenario advances the causal explanation rather than merely
  repeating it.
- The four-question demonstration reveals four distinct omissions and ends with a
  conditional, inspectable decision process.
- The narration includes a real boundary and at least two brief transfer contexts.
- The last sentence resolves the opening question and preserves the approved final line.
- No new factual specificity is invented.

## Non-goals

- Do not change the episode's thesis, title, or four-question payoff.
- Do not turn the episode into a general survey of AI bias, prompting, hallucinations, or
  alignment.
- Do not add more named phenomena merely to fill runtime.
- Do not update the canonical launch sequence or declare this script the replacement pilot;
  that remains a separate unresolved editorial decision.
- Do not perform production audits before Martin sees the complete narration.
