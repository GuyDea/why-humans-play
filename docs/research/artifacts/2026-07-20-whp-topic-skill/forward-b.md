# WHP Next-Video Recommendation

## Decision

**Decision status:** Winner selected  
**Winner:** App streaks — why a counter turns a useful habit into a rule you obey, and how to keep the motivation without letting one missed day erase the goal  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Why it wins now:** It combines a globally familiar hidden game, unusually strong primary evidence, an immediate visual contradiction, and a five-day production path without depending on a decaying news spike.  
**Strongest honest title/thumbnail promise:** **Your Streak Is Playing You** — a `365🔥` counter snapping to `0` across one missed calendar square, promising to explain why that feels like losing everything and how to redesign the rule.  
**Mode:** cold-start

This is a comparative editorial judgment, not a view forecast. The scorecard ranks the available evidence under the stated frame; it does not guarantee performance.

## Candidate landscape

**Pool:** 36 distinct subjects across current signals, explicit games, play/learning, everyday institutions, and digital/social systems. **Shallow-scan survivors:** 10 exact angles. **Deep-research finalists:** 5. **Conspicuous trend-bait angles retained and rejected:** 2 (the broad AI slogan and a post-event World Cup penalty rush).

Gate key: `G` game/play centrality, `H` human revelation, `P` recognized payoff, `E` evidence path, `R` production reality, `F` portfolio fit. `✓` is pass and `✗` is fail. Every advancing angle passed all six; failed angles were not scored.

**AI evidence ruling.** The collaborator-reported 20M views make the unnamed video a research lead, not a demand estimate: without the URL, upload age, channel baseline, format, traffic source, package, and satisfaction data, it is one particular outcome with no valid transfer denominator (R4). Likewise, Google Trends `100` is the normalized high point inside a chosen query/geography/window/comparison, not an absolute demand level; the missing configuration and brief duration make the report non-comparable and liable to decay before publication (R5). Those two facts do not justify “AI is changing everything.” The narrower “games are an honest test for AI agents” angle is evidentially viable (R7–R8), but it is an explicit-games/technology episode and fails the next-pilot portfolio requirement (R1–R2).

## Winner brief

| Exact subject and angle | **App streaks — why a counter turns a useful habit into a rule you obey, and how to keep the motivation without letting one missed day erase the goal.** |
| Working logline | A streak can genuinely help people return, but once continuity becomes the score, one arbitrary break can feel more important than the activity it was meant to support. |
| Intended viewer | An English-speaking viewer who uses a language, fitness, social, reading, meditation, or habit app and has felt protective of—or defeated by—a day counter. |
| Why now | Primarily **evergreen/growth**, not news-driven: streaks are already a large, cross-category behavior, with current first-party scale and recent research. That makes the five-day deadline an execution constraint rather than a race against a spike (R9–R16). |
| Familiar entry point and central tension | Entry: the flame, the daily notification, and the fear of returning to zero. Tension: when does a consistency tool stop serving the real goal and become a separate game whose loss state controls the player? |
| Game/play mechanism | A visible score, daily move, continuity rule, loss state, recovery affordance, and status/identity feedback reshape the next decision—even when underlying behavior is similar (R10–R14). |
| Human revelation and stakes | People do not respond only to accumulated progress; they respond to how a system frames continuity, loss, and identity. That can support persistence, but it can also turn a small lapse into abandonment. |

| Strongest reach evidence and competitive opening | Reach: approximately 43M Duolingo DAUs had 7+ day streaks and about 15M had 365+ day streaks at 2025 year-end (R9), while a related TED/Duolingo lesson page displayed about 11.0M views when checked (R15).
