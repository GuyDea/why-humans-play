# Research Summary: Choosing WHP's Next YouTube Topic

- **Date:** 2026-07-20
- **Question:** What repeatable process is most likely to identify a high-reach next video
  topic that remains coherent with Why Humans Play?
- **Conclusion:** Use a staged evidence funnel. Generate broadly from independent signals,
  convert subjects into specific promises, gate for WHP/rigor/payoff/feasibility, triangulate
  demand and competition, stress-test packaging, and choose one winner with explicit
  uncertainty only when a responsible comparison exists. Otherwise return an explicit
  incomplete result. No single keyword, trend, outlier, or AI suggestion can answer the question.

## Executive findings

### 1. Optimize for an audience response, not an imagined algorithm

YouTube describes recommendation performance through three response buckets: **appeal**
(whether viewers choose the video), **engagement** (whether they continue watching), and
**satisfaction** (whether they enjoyed the result). Its current recommendation guidance says
the system aims to help each viewer find content they want and maximize long-term
satisfaction.

**Application:** Topic research cannot stop at demand. A finalist needs a package people
choose, a tension that can sustain attention, and a payoff that honestly satisfies the
promise. The skill should test these before scripting.

Sources:

- [YouTube: recommendation system](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387)
- [YouTube: content performance for recommendations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)
- [YouTube: how recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387)

### 2. Topic size, competition, and timing are separate external factors

YouTube explicitly distinguishes topic interest, competition, and seasonality. A broadly
interesting topic may still face prohibitive competition; a narrow topic may have a clean
opening; a normally weak subject may become timely around an event.

**Application:** Score reachable audience and competitive opening separately. Record whether
the case is evergreen, steadily rising, seasonal, or a short-lived spike. Compare candidates
against what else the intended viewer could watch, not only against WHP's own backlog.

Source:

- [YouTube: external recommendation factors](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16558238)

### 3. First-party audience data should lead when it is meaningful

YouTube Studio's Trends tab exposes top searches, breakout videos from similar creators,
recent videos watched around saved topics, audience-interest indicators, and Shorts content
gaps. Those Shorts gaps are an adjacent demand clue rather than direct proof that a long-form
episode will work.
The Audience tab exposes videos and channels WHP viewers watch elsewhere. Advanced Mode can
compare topic or format groups across equal portions of their lifespan.

**Application:** Once WHP has enough audience history, use these as the leading
audience-specific signals. Until then, use a cold-start workflow instead of pretending sparse
analytics are representative.

Sources:

- [YouTube: Trends tab](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)
- [YouTube: what viewers are watching](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616337)
- [YouTube: Audience reports](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416)
- [YouTube: Advanced Mode comparisons](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491)
- [YouTube: new, casual, and regular viewers](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996)

### 4. Search and trend data reveal demand, but not total opportunity

YouTube search ranks for relevance, engagement, and quality; its results are not simply a
list of the most-viewed videos. Google Trends offers a YouTube Search mode, but its values are
sampled, normalized within the selected time and geography, and scaled from 0 to 100. Google
warns that low-volume data can contain noise and that Trends should be one input among
others.

**Application:** Use Trends to compare direction, seasonality, phrasing, and relative interest
under consistent settings. Never report its index as absolute search volume. Search demand
also cannot measure browse/suggested potential, so it must be paired with audience, outlier,
package, and payoff evidence.

Sources:

- [YouTube: how search works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438)
- [Google News Initiative: Advanced Google Trends](https://newsinitiative.withgoogle.com/resources/trainings/google-trends/advanced-google-trends/)
- [Google Trends data FAQ](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533)
- [Google Trends comparison guidance](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4359550)

### 5. Relative outliers are useful hypotheses; raw views are misleading

An outlier compares a video's performance with the normal performance of its own channel.
This is more informative than raw views because a modest total can be exceptional for a small
channel while a large total can underperform for a large one. Commercial research tools now
surface outlier score, views per hour, format, and date filters.

Outliers still confound the topic, title, thumbnail, execution, audience, timing, and external
events. Public lifetime views also favor older videos and growing channels.

**Application:** Compare the same format, use similar age windows, prefer a recent rolling
channel baseline, and look for repeated patterns across several creators. Treat an outlier as
a reason to investigate an angle—not as proof to copy it.

Sources:

- [vidIQ: Outliers documentation](https://support.vidiq.com/en/articles/9660010-outliers)
- [YouTube: Trends breakout videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)

### 6. A topic is not yet a video idea

YouTube's own ideation guidance recommends framing specialist material through a universal,
relatable entry point. Its thumbnail experiments choose winners using watch-time share rather
than click-through rate alone, because a good package should attract the right viewer and set
accurate expectations.

**Application:** Convert every promising subject into an explicit combination of familiar
entry point, game/play mechanism, human stake, and earned payoff. Stress-test multiple
title/thumbnail promises before the final ranking. Reject attractive packages that the
proposed video cannot satisfy.

Sources:

- [YouTube: content performance and universal framing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)
- [YouTube: Test & Compare thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)

### 7. Useful, surprising, and activating payoffs are more shareable priors

Berger and Milkman's field and experimental research found that useful, interesting, and
surprising content was more likely to be shared, and that high-arousal emotions such as awe
were associated with greater transmission. The field setting was New York Times articles,
not YouTube, so this is a content-design prior rather than a platform-specific law.

**Application:** Score whether the payoff is useful, surprising, or emotionally activating,
but never manufacture anger, anxiety, or spectacle. WHP's strongest natural share drivers are
likely awe, intellectual surprise, practical reframing, and the pleasure of making an
invisible rule legible.

Source:

- [Berger & Milkman, “What Makes Online Content Viral?”](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/pandemic-teaching-initiative/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2020/09/What-Makes-Online-Content-Viral.pdf)

### 8. Generate beyond the obvious, and do not rely on AI-only diversity

The “creative cliff” research found that people tend to expect creativity to decline during
continued ideation even though it often persists or improves, causing them to stop too early.
Recent research also finds that ChatGPT can improve average idea quality while reducing the
diversity of an idea pool.

**Application:** Require a broad candidate pool, continue past the first obvious ideas, and
seed candidates independently from channel files, platform data, communities, research,
history, actual games, and current events. Use deliberate WHP lanes so the model does not
produce many paraphrases of the same fashionable concept.

Sources:

- [Lucas & Nordgren, “The creative cliff illusion”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7443938/)
- [Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch, “ChatGPT decreases idea diversity in brainstorming”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02173-x)

### 9. Broad subject matter needs a stable audience promise

The user-provided planning conversation produced a useful articulation: WHP should be an
ideas channel where games are the lens through which humanity is explained. It also usefully
separated many candidate territories—evolution, intelligence, civilization, biology, AI,
education, society, philosophy, history, psychology, and future play.

The conversation's fixed recommendations, star scores, and sweeping claims were not based on
the current evidence pass and should not be imported as facts.

**Application:** Use the topic territories to diversify candidate generation and the
“games/play as a lens on humanity” sentence as a coherence test. Treat bold phrases such as
“games are the operating system of intelligence” as hypotheses that must earn their scope.
Require two plausible follow-up episodes from each finalist so broad reach builds a coherent
viewer journey rather than a collection of unrelated hits.

Source:

- [User-provided ChatGPT conversation: “Games and Human Impact”](https://chatgpt.com/share/6a5dec42-1e54-83ed-bac1-4f434ffe8add)

## Recommended evidence funnel

### Stage A: establish the frame

Read current brand/channel doctrine, episode state, and production constraints. Define the
market, language, publication window, and what “next” means in the current sequence.

### Stage B: collect independent signals

Use a portfolio of sources:

| Signal | What it can show | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| WHP analytics | Actual audience response and adjacency | Future performance of a new subject |
| Studio Trends/Audience | Searches, watched topics, breakouts, Shorts gap clues | Complete market demand or long-form opportunity |
| Google Trends, YouTube mode | Relative direction, timing, phrasing | Absolute YouTube volume or browse demand |
| Public YouTube results | Existing supply, packages, dates, visible performance | Private impressions, CTR, retention, satisfaction |
| Relative outliers | Unusual performance worth studying | That topic alone caused the breakout |
| Comments/forums/communities | Questions, emotion, language, misconceptions | Representative audience size |
| News/research/calendar | Timeliness and authoritative novelty | Durable viewer interest |
| WHP backlog and doctrine | Strategic coherence and accumulated insight | Current external demand |

### Stage C: diverge before evaluating

Generate at least 30 subjects across distinct WHP lanes, then create multiple angles for the
promising subjects. Keep generation separate from scoring.

### Stage D: gate and narrow

Reject candidates lacking game/play centrality, a human revelation, recognized payoff,
credible evidence path, feasibility, or portfolio distinction. Run a shallow scan to reduce
the pool before expensive deep research.

### Stage E: triangulate finalists

For roughly five finalists, require multiple independent demand/opportunity signals and
research the factual backbone. Grade evidence and record counterevidence.

### Stage F: score and package

Use the approved 100-point rubric:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Audience demand and reachable market | 25 |
| Competitive opening or content gap | 15 |
| Packaging strength and immediate appeal | 20 |
| Likely satisfaction, usefulness, and shareability | 15 |
| Strength of the WHP lens | 10 |
| Evidence quality and defensibility | 10 |
| Production feasibility and sequence | 5 |

Then create three distinct title/thumbnail promises for each top-three finalist. Re-rank if
the package cannot honestly express and deliver the idea.

### Stage G: decide and expose uncertainty

Return one winner and explain why the runner-up lost when at least two responsibly supported
finalists remain. If only one or none remains, state that no winner is responsibly supportable,
identify any sole supported finalist separately, and list the evidence needed for a valid
comparison. State what could change the decision and identify factual claims requiring
verification before scripting. A lower-confidence answer is acceptable; fabricated certainty is not.

## Common failure modes the skill should prevent

| Failure | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Recommending a broad noun such as “Sudoku” | Require a tension, human stake, and earned payoff |
| Chasing whatever is currently popular | Apply WHP and payoff gates before scoring |
| Calling Google Trends values “search volume” | Label them normalized relative interest |
| Comparing raw views across unequal channels | Use same-format, age-aware relative baselines |
| Copying one breakout | Seek repeated patterns and independent demand evidence |
| Generating many near-duplicate AI ideas | Seed from independent sources and enforce topic lanes |
| Choosing clickability over delivery | Stress-test package/payoff alignment |
| Letting breadth turn into channel randomness | Apply the human-through-games coherence and adjacency tests |
| Returning five “great options” | Require one winner and runner-up comparison when supported; otherwise require an explicit incomplete/no-winner result |
| Inventing unavailable analytics | Mark unknown, switch research mode, and lower confidence |

## How this research changes the skill

The skill should be a research and decision procedure, not a static list of promising WHP
topics. Its durable value comes from:

- reading current repository doctrine rather than freezing brand assumptions;
- adapting between cold-start and channel-aware evidence;
- generating candidates across broad but explicit WHP territories;
- forcing subject-to-angle development;
- triangulating demand, competition, packaging, satisfaction, and evidence;
- exposing provenance and uncertainty; and
- making one reviewable editorial decision.

That process is slower than asking an AI for “ten viral topics,” but it directly targets the
failure modes that make such lists generic, homogeneous, difficult to verify, and hard to act
on.
