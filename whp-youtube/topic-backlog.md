## Candidates

### 1. The hobby you monetized
- **Nerve:** "I turned what I love into income and now I can't love it."
- **Gap:** breaks the near-universal "do what you love" model; side-hustle culture makes
  it personal for almost everyone.
- **Proof case:** Lepper, Greene & Nisbett 1973 — preschoolers who loved drawing were
  promised a "Good Player Award"; rewarded children later drew far less in free play
  (overjustification effect). Primary PDF: Stanford Bing School archive.
- **WHP lens:** the exact mechanism by which play collapses into work.
- **Hook sketch:** "Getting paid for what you love might be how you stop loving it."

### 2. The 15 minutes
- **Nerve:** "I can't sit doing nothing without reaching for my phone."
- **Gap:** nobody knows their own number; the documented fact is absurd on first hearing.
- **Proof case:** Wilson et al. 2014, *Science* — many participants self-administered
  electric shocks (which they had said they'd pay to avoid) rather than sit alone with
  their thoughts for 15 minutes; one outlier shocked himself 190 times.
- **WHP lens:** boredom as the engine that makes play necessary.
- **Hook sketch:** "How long could you sit alone with your thoughts before you'd rather
  hurt yourself?"

### 3. The game that patches brains
- **Nerve:** the settled model says games rot your brain; this runs the other way.
- **Gap:** a positive reversal — a game as a medical intervention.
- **Proof case:** Holmes et al. (Oxford) — Tetris shortly after trauma reduced intrusive
  flashbacks; emergency-department proof-of-concept RCT (*Molecular Psychiatry* 2017);
  "cognitive vaccine" framing. Built-in honesty beat: a 2025 multilab replication found
  the effect on immediate but not later intrusions — science self-correcting on camera.
- **WHP lens:** explicit-game lane; what a game mechanically does to a mind.
- **Hook sketch:** "There's a video game hospitals have used on car-crash victims. On
  purpose."

### 4. Why you didn't study — RETURNED TO POOL, RE-SCOPED (2026-08-01)

> Was EP2 (selected 2026-07-29); superseded when the coverage check showed
> self-handicapping owns a minority of the "night you didn't study" moment. The
> verified final pair survives at `episodes/ep002-why-you-didnt-study/final/` with
> ten reusable evidence records. Honest re-scope for any future slot: self-sabotage
> **after unexplained success and with an audience** (Berglas & Jones 1978 male-only
> lab result; effect largely disappears in private, Kolditz & Arkin 1982) — not a
> universal diagnosis of procrastination. The nerve below predates that re-scope.
- **Nerve:** everyone remembers not preparing for something that mattered and never
  understanding why.
- **Gap:** the viewer's model has no answer for deliberate self-sabotage; the mundane
  explanations (laziness) don't survive the evidence.
- **Proof case:** Berglas & Jones 1978 — after success that felt unearned, students
  offered a choice before a retest picked the performance-*impairing* drug; losing the
  stated game to protect the identity story ("it was the drug, not me").
- **WHP lens:** self-handicapping as a hidden game played against yourself — trading the
  outcome game to win the excuse game.
- **Hook sketch:** "People will choose to fail — on purpose — to protect a story."

### 5. The referee's crowd
- **Nerve:** every fan is certain refs favor the home team, and wrong about why home
  advantage exists.
- **Gap:** the felt model (crowd energizes players) can't produce the observed result.
- **Proof case:** COVID empty-stadium natural experiment — home advantage fell by half or
  more across Bundesliga/EPL/Serie A/La Liga, and referee bias (cards, added time)
  largely vanished without crowds (Economics Letters 2020; PLOS One systematic review).
- **WHP lens:** the hidden game (social pressure on the official) inside the explicit one.
- **Hook sketch:** "Home advantage is real. It just isn't the players who feel it."

### 6. Your invisible score
- **Nerve:** "apps are ranking me and I can't see the number."
- **Proof case:** Tinder's internal Elo desirability score (circa 2012–2019, confirmed
  and retired by Tinder's own engineering post); a Fast Company journalist saw her score
  and wished she hadn't. Chess Elo history supplies depth.
- **WHP lens:** hidden rating systems as the matchmaking rules of modern life.
- **Hook sketch:** "There was a number attached to you. You were never allowed to see it."

### 7. Paying to suffer
- **Nerve:** "why do I love what hurts — spicy food, sad songs, brutally hard games?"
- **Proof case:** Rozin et al., "Glad to be sad" (*Judgment and Decision Making* 2013) —
  29 initially aversive experiences reliably converted to pleasure; "mind over body"
  mechanism (benign masochism).
- **WHP lens:** play as safely borrowed danger.
- **Hook sketch:** "You regularly pay for experiences your own brain classifies as
  threats."

### 8. Your miles are someone's bank
- **Nerve:** fascination + money stake: "what are my points actually worth, and to whom?"
- **Proof case:** 2020 — United pledged MileagePlus for a $5B loan; the program was
  valued around $20–22B while the airline was worth roughly $10B; ~$5.3B of miles sold in
  2019 (Skift/FT analyses).
- **WHP lens:** a points game where the scorekeeper can reprice the points at will.
- **Hook sketch:** "The miles in your account are worth more than the airline."

### 9. The buttons that do nothing
- **Nerve:** light but delicious — the world hands you fake controls.
- **Proof case:** most NYC crosswalk buttons deactivated since the late 1980s (~1,000
  dead); elevator door-close buttons non-functional for the public; 2003 industry survey
  with 72% admitting dummy thermostats. Ties to illusion-of-control research (Langer).
- **WHP lens:** illusion of control as civic and app design.
- **Note:** possibly stronger as a Shorts series than a flagship; payoff is thin for
  long-form.
- **Hook sketch:** "You've been pressing buttons that were never connected to anything."

### 10. The play deficit
- **Nerve:** "is it really the screens?" (parent-side).
- **Proof case:** play-deprived rats show impaired prefrontal development and worse
  stress/social outcomes (J. Neuroscience 2022; Panksepp line); human decline-of-play ↔
  anxiety correlation (Gray 2011, *American Journal of Play*) — correlational, and must
  be said so.
- **WHP lens:** the channel-name answer — play as developmental infrastructure.
- **Note:** crowded shelf (Haidt-adjacent) and correlational human evidence; strongest
  later, not as EP2.
