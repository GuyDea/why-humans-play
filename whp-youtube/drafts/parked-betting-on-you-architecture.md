## Premise

A sportsbook doesn't run a contest against you; it runs a sorting machine over you — it
measures every customer, expels the few whose bets carry skill, and cultivates everyone
else with products and promotions tuned to how profitably they lose.

- **Central question:** when you bet on a game, what game are you actually in — and why
  does getting good at it get you thrown out?
- **Hook direction (rubric-tested):** "If you get good at sports betting, they stop
  letting you play."
- **Mode:** The Hidden Game.

## Insight ladder (summary)

1. **The product mutated; the "fun" bet is the expensive one.** Parlay hold ≈ 4–5×
   straight bets (IL 2023: 18.2% vs 4.9%; NJ Sep 2024: 24.2% vs 4.4%); same-game parlays
   ≈ 35–40% of gross revenue (industry estimate).
2. **The feeling of skill is a manufactured input.** Illusion of control (Langer 1975;
   Clark & Wohl 2022 review): choice, familiarity, involvement cues — all supplied by
   parlay-building and push-prompted live betting.
3. **The machine fires winners and waters losers.** Operators on record defending limits
   on sharp customers (ESPN; WY/MA regulator scrutiny); UK 2020 code had to ban
   incentivizing customers based on losses; 8% of ~47k online VIPs were problem gamblers;
   schemes fell ~90–95% once executives became accountable. Boundary: operators dispute
   "limited for winning" (one reports ~half of limited customers were net losers).
4. **The sort has a body count; willpower tools aren't what works.** Online legalization
   → ~25% bankruptcy increase (UCLA/USC/Harvard, working-paper stage); deposit-limit
   uptake ~0.3% in one 47k dataset; best RCT result came from banning operator marketing
   contact for 9 months.

## Earned reframe

"Responsible gambling" as personal-discipline framing misdescribes an industrial system
whose most profitable customers are its least in-control; the operator holds the tuning
dial. Predicts the same two-sided sort anywhere an app profits from measurable
engagement (f2p whales, gamified trading, escalating loyalty programs). The tell is
asymmetric treatment.

## Learning-and-action contract (summary)

Read every promotion as a classification file: name the behavior it pays you to repeat,
check its hold class (state dashboards are public), ask whether you'd do the behavior
unpaid. Observable result: you can predict your own next offer. Boundaries: reading the
sort doesn't beat the edge; being limited doesn't prove winning; this lens is not
addiction treatment.
