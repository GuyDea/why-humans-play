## Packaging-patterns brief — AI-advice danger topic — 2026-07-30

- **Landscape:** ~50 videos / ~35 channels swept by two research agents (same-topic:
  AI-advice harm; adjacent: cognitive-bias/manipulation education). The feed
  neighbors we'll sit next to most often: Eddy Burback "ChatGPT made me delusional"
  (5.6M), More Perfect Union "We Investigated AI Psychosis…" (2.9M), Veritasium
  psych-cluster videos (6–17M).
- **Outliers (≥3x, same topic):** Justin Sung "How ChatGPT Slowly Destroys Your
  Brain" (1.0M, ~10x); More Perfect Union "We Investigated AI Psychosis. What We
  Found Will Shock You" (2.9M, ~3.7x); Firstpost "5 Things You Should Never Ask
  ChatGPT" (2.1M, ~5–10x est.); Doctor Mike "Asking ChatGPT Tough Medical Questions"
  (2.2M, ~3.5x); penguinz0 Character.AI tragedy (3.9M, ~3.3x). Category-definer:
  Eddy Burback (5.6M, template-setter). Adjacent mega-outliers: Better Than
  Yesterday "How I Tricked My Brain…" (28.5M, ~20x); Sprouts "The Pygmalion Effect"
  (19.4M); After Skool "MASS PSYCHOSIS" (8.7M, ~25x).
- **Winning patterns (clustered, ≥2 channels each):**
  - **S1 First-person harm confession, statement not question** — "ChatGPT made me
    delusional"; "AI 'Therapist' Told me to KILL". AI is subject, human is
    object; the title is the ending, curiosity is *how*.
  - **S2 Second-person present-tense harm with concrete body nouns** — "How ChatGPT
    Slowly Destroys Your Brain"; "ChatGPT is lying to you..".
  - **S3 Prohibition/never-ask with unresolved referent** — "5 Things You Should
    Never Ask ChatGPT"; "Never Ask ChatGPT This".
  - **S4 Investigation reveal, two-sentence, withheld finding** — "We Investigated
    AI Psychosis. What We Found Will Shock You".
  - **A2 Trick/fool verbs + named agent** — "How I Tricked My Brain…"; "How Brands
    Use Design to Control Your Mind".
  - **A3 Smart-people negation / counterintuitive inversion** — "Why Smart People
    Believe Stupid Things"; "Why People Are So Confident When They're Wrong"; "Why
    Being Delusional is a Superpower".
  - **A6 Number + vivid metaphor beats jargon** — "21 Mind Traps" (7.4M) vs "12
    Cognitive Biases Explained" (2.8M) vs "The Anchoring Bias" (36K).
  - Cross-cutting: name the product ("ChatGPT", 8 of top 10) not "AI chatbots";
    violent concrete verbs; two documented retitle case studies (Veritasium, CGP
    Grey) moved *from* challenge-questions *to* superlative statements.
- **Anti-patterns:** neutral genuine questions ("Can you trust ChatGPT health…?" →
  8–15K); research jargon — the entire "sycophancy"-titled cohort is under 3K views;
  third-person news voice ("Man alleges…"); hedged conditionals; "X Signs You're
  Being Manipulated" commodity frame (~200–300K plateau).
- **Thumbnail conventions:** the niche signature is the **chat receipt as evidence**
  (Burback's chat bubbles, Doctor Mike's Q/A block) — screenshots-as-proof beat
  robot art; OpenAI logo recolored as villain; distressed human face; short
  second-person overlay that extends (never repeats) the title; dark ground +
  red/pink alarm accents. Premium adjacent channels (Veritasium/Kurzgesagt) stay
  cleaner: face+object+2–5-word claim, or bright flat art against the genre's dark
  clutter.
- **Competitor thumbnails:** `mockups/competitors/` (8 saved, with title sidecars).
- **The open gap (both agents, independently):** nobody big owns "the chatbot
  flatters your hope / tells you what you want to hear" as packaging — the audience
  vocabulary for it is "lying to you", "glazing", "made me delusional", never
  "sycophancy". Ep001's thesis sits exactly in this gap.
- **Confidence:** view counts exact at fetch (2026-07-30); channel baselines are
  recent-upload medians (noisy; Firstpost weakest); era baselines rough; thumbnail
  conventions medium (inspected for same-topic top 10, from knowledge for adjacent).
