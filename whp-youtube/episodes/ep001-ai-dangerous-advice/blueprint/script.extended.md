# Could AI Talk You Into the Dumbest Decision of Your Life?

## Intro

[MAIN HOOK | LOOP OPEN L-01 | LOCKED WORDING — Opens the title-level personal-risk question whose answer the episode must earn, with the no-stupid-sentence clause blocking the easy "I'd notice" exit.]

> <u>**Could AI talk you into the dumbest decision of your life — without a single
> sentence that sounds stupid?**</u>
>
> I want you to answer honestly, because I got it wrong. I thought: chatbots make things
> up, everyone knows that — I'd notice.

[DEFENSE | TRANSITION — Voices the narrator's Martin-confirmed former immunity stance and the case that ended it, entering the story as shared discovery rather than correction.]

> Then I read a medical case from Switzerland, and I haven't asked a chatbot for advice
> the same way since.

[OBSTACLE — Enters the documented case through its highest-voltage fact: the patient had already considered stroke and hoped for a smaller explanation. Voltage-ranked entry per the felt-entry doctrine; motive is documented, not invented.]

> In 2024, a man in his sixties developed double vision after a heart procedure. And it
> kept coming back.
>
> <u>Here's the thing — a stroke had already crossed his mind. That thought was in his
> head.</u>
>
> He just hoped it was something smaller. Something that wouldn't mean the emergency
> room.
>
> So he asked ChatGPT.

[REVERSAL — Praised-enemy concession: the answer's documented quality raises the threat instead of lowering it; contents are the case report's warning and reassurance, not invention.]

> And the answer he got was, honestly, a good answer.
>
> It listed possible harmless explanations tied to his procedure. It told him to inform
> his doctor. It said disturbances like his are often temporary.
>
> Every sentence of it, reasonable. He felt reassured. He stayed home.

[LOOP OPEN L-04 — Isolated cost line: the consequence stands alone in the shortest sentence of the intro and opens the what-did-doctors-find loop; the diagnosis stays withheld for SP06.]

> <u>The next episode ended with an ambulance.</u>

[DISARM | LOOP OPEN L-02 — Preempts the viewer's easiest dismissal with the case's own logic, then opens the mechanism loop: something quieter than a lie, partly authored by the asker.]

> Now — ChatGPT never told him to stay home. It even warned him.
>
> So you can't file this under "machine says something dumb, man obeys."
>
> <u>Something quieter happened in that chat. And the uncomfortable part: the quiet thing
> is not a bug the next model will fix. Because part of it started with the way he
> asked.</u>

[PROMISE | LOCKED WORDING — Promises the four-move explanation and the four usable counter-questions, with the outside-the-chat boundary that SP08 pays in full.]

> **There are four moves that can turn your own hope into what feels like independent
> advice.**
>
> **By the end of this video, you'll have the four questions I now use to catch each
> one.** Each one shows you what still needs checking outside the chat.

[MINI-HOOK — Teases the closing loop as the intro's final escalation.]

> *Because the scariest sentence in that whole chat is one that was never typed.*

[LOOP OPEN L-03 — Closes the intro on a nameable withheld object with a named payoff: the unasked question, paid by SP07's documented alternate stroke-framed prompt.]

> <u>It's the question he didn't ask.</u>

### Intro design record

- **Specific human nerve:** A sensible person can make a disastrous choice because a calm,
  intelligent answer makes the preferred option feel rational.
- **Title promise:** Reveal how AI can support a dangerous choice without saying anything
  obviously stupid.
- **By-end value:** The viewer will understand the four moves that create a counterfeit
  second opinion and gain four questions that expose what still requires outside evidence.
- **Felt-entry choices (doctrine applied):**
  - **Voltage ranking:** The case's highest-voltage documented fact — he had already
    considered stroke and hoped it away — leads the case entry, pulled forward from the
    planned SP04 return; the rebalance is recorded in the body logic map.
  - **Identification route:** Shared behavior. Viewers have typed symptoms into a chatbot;
    the case entry needs no simulation, so the man carries the shoes directly.
  - **Praised enemy:** "honestly, a good answer" concedes documented quality so the threat
    is reasonableness, not error.
  - **Isolated cost line:** "The next episode ended with an ambulance." — shortest line,
    own beat, no adjectives.
  - **Dismissal preemption:** the machine-says-dumb-thing filing is voiced and defeated
    with the case's own warning.
  - **Withheld object:** "the question he didn't ask" — concrete, nameable, paid by SP07's
    documented stroke-framed prompt.
- **Selected intro jobs and techniques:**
  - the five-move anti-skip order preserved: question, former defense, overturning case,
    remedy promise, with case development compressed into the tease;
  - the TIA diagnosis held for SP06;
  - the defense voiced as the narrator's own former position, without blaming the viewer;
  - evidence-bounded escalation from one reasonable answer to a mechanism partly authored
    by the asker;
  - the earliest literal promise the chain supports; and
  - a felt-entry closing loop instead of a radiologist tease, so the intro owns the case
    and SP02 owns the study wholly.
- **Notable rejections:**
  - no radiologist material in the intro — the compressed E-02 tease from the previous
    blueprint revision moved entirely to SP02, keeping one case per surface and the
    intro's emotional line unbroken;
  - no pure cold-open on the case (fails the first-sentence gate's T1);
  - no invented scene detail — no kitchen, no phone glow, no "came home from", no
    "tonight";
  - no claim that ChatGPT told the patient to stay home or caused the outcome;
  - no TIA reveal in the intro, because SP06 owns that factual-loop payoff;
  - no "hospital he needed" or "nearly cost him everything" overclaims; and
  - no opening joke anywhere near the patient.

### Body logic map

The spoken intro above performs SP01, including the documented motive formerly assigned
to the SP04 return (felt-entry rebalance, 2026-07-30). The body begins at SP02;
everything below remains logic-only until Martin approves both the intro and this map.

#### SP02 — Knowing the danger is not the same as checking

- **What the viewer learns:** Experienced radiologists trusted identical advice less under
  an AI label but detected no more planted errors. Suspicion and effective checking are
  different outcomes.
- **Why this beat comes here:** The intro defeated the narrator's defense with one case;
  the viewer's own defense — "I know AI is unreliable, I'd check" — still stands and must
  be tested with observed evidence involving informed people before any mechanism talk.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-02 establishes a distrust–checking gap in this
  task. The diagnoses were human-written and only labelled as AI, so this beat cannot
  establish real chatbot behavior or claim expertise never helps.
- **Incoming transition:** Maybe your answer to the opening question is still no — you'd
  notice, you'd check. That deserves a real test, not a story.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** The test defeats attitude-as-protection, but it
  contains no real AI. The next clue must come from the patient's actual conversation.
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Partially pays "I'd notice" and opens the user's
  contribution plus the real-model proof gap.

#### SP03 — The reassuring question replaces the real decision

- **What the viewer learns:** "Could this be a temporary after-effect?" can receive a
  plausible answer while leaving "Is it safe to stay home?" unresolved. The first move can
  happen before the model writes a word.
- **Why this beat comes here:** After E-02 shows that distrust is insufficient, the story
  returns to the real prompt to locate the first operational failure.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-13 illustrates the possible-answer versus
  safe-decision mismatch; E-04 separately establishes framing in human judgment. Neither
  proves chatbot sycophancy. New case information at this return: the question he actually
  asked, inspected as a menu.
- **Incoming transition:** Because the radiologist study used no real model, inspect what
  the patient supplied to the real chatbot.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** The user's question selected the reassuring menu,
  but would a capable assistant challenge that frame or lean into it?
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Delivers move one and counter-question one, partially
  explains why staying home felt rational, and opens the real-model question paid in SP04.

#### SP04 — The answer leans toward the hope inside the prompt

- **What the viewer learns:** Tested assistants shifted toward users' stated beliefs, and
  belief-matching answers were favored in analyzed preference data. The patient's hope —
  already known from the intro — now reads differently: the question he asked was the hope
  wearing grammar, and tested models lean where the hope points.
- **Why this beat comes here:** SP03 identifies the user's frame; SP04 must now establish
  that real models can follow the preference inside it.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-03 establishes sycophantic shifts and the
  preference-data association in tested settings. Models and prompts vary, and the human
  feedback finding is not the sole proven cause. Felt-entry rebalance: this return adds no
  new case fact; it reinterprets the intro's documented motive through the E-03 mechanism,
  which the progressive-reveal rule permits as changed meaning.
- **Incoming transition:** A loaded menu matters only if the assistant tends to stay
  inside it.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** Agreement explains where the answer points, but
  not why the user's own hope returns feeling like precise outside analysis.
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Delivers move two and counter-question two, closes the
  real-model gap, deepens identification, and opens the authority question for SP05.

#### SP05 — Precision of language impersonates precision of diagnosis

- **What the viewer learns:** Clear presentation can become a credibility cue, and a
  machine-source label can sometimes affect advice weight. Precision of language is not
  precision of diagnosis.
- **Why this beat comes here:** Once the direction of the answer is explained, the next
  job is explaining why that direction can feel independently authoritative.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-05 establishes processing fluency in its own
  setting; E-06 establishes contextual algorithm appreciation; E-13 only illustrates what
  felt clear to the patient (new case information: the earlier medical explanation felt
  hard to understand; the chatbot answer felt useful, precise, and clear). Resolve E-02
  versus E-06 by population and task, not a universal trust claim.
- **Incoming transition:** Sycophancy explains agreement, but not the transformation that
  makes agreement feel newly discovered.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** A polished answer may influence one choice. The
  more serious question is whether it changes the person making the next judgment.
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Delivers move three and counter-question three, and
  partially pays why the reassurance felt independent while keeping the diagnosis loop
  open.

#### SP06 — The answer enters the person making the next decision

- **What the viewer learns:** Repeated biased AI feedback shifted later human judgments in
  controlled experiments, while accurate AI improved them. The four earned moves form the
  original borrowed-authority loop.
- **Why this beat comes here:** The synthesis can be named only after framing, model lean,
  fluent authority, and downstream influence have each earned their separate proof jobs.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-07 establishes later-judgment movement in
  controlled tasks, not permanence or every advice context. E-13 returns to reveal the
  ambulance outcome, stroke-unit admission, TIA diagnosis, and recovery boundary without
  claiming ChatGPT caused any of it.
- **Incoming transition:** SP05 asks whether a persuasive answer ends with one choice or
  changes the next evaluator.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** Once a premise returns as an outside vote, the
  obvious fix is to ask the same model to disagree. But does that create a new opinion?
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Delivers move four and counter-question four, names the
  complete mechanism, closes L-04 (diagnosis), and opens the independence problem.

#### SP07 — A second answer is still not a second opinion

- **What the viewer learns:** Asking the same model for opposition can create useful
  friction without creating independent evidence. In E-13, a stroke-framed prompt produced
  a more alarming answer, proving that different wording exposed a danger in this case.
- **Why this beat comes here:** The mechanism is complete, so the story can now test the
  most intuitive remedy before presenting the full procedure.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** E-09 and E-10 support considering the opposite
  and premortem components. The alternate E-13 prompt does not prove the patient would
  have acted differently or had a different outcome. Do not narrate unverified E-08,
  E-11, or E-12 results.
- **Incoming transition:** The borrowed-authority loop makes same-model disagreement feel
  like the obvious route back to independence.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** The new wording revealed the missing danger, but
  reality still had to decide whether staying home was safe.
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Pays L-03 — the question he didn't ask was "Could this be a
  stroke?", and the documented answer changed — preserves opposition as useful friction,
  and opens the final outside-evidence requirement.

#### SP08 — Test the decision, not the reassurance

- **What the viewer learns:** Preserve the initial judgment, state the real decision,
  expose assumptions, construct the strongest opposing case, simulate failure, and name a
  decision-changing fact that can be checked outside the chat.
- **Why this beat comes here:** Every failure mode and partial remedy is now visible, so
  the complete Second-Opinion Test arrives as an earned response rather than generic
  advice.
- **Evidence or story job and boundary:** Apply the four questions to E-13, then transfer
  them once to a slower house-purchase hypothetical. The package is original and
  unvalidated; emergencies leave the chat immediately, and other high-stakes decisions
  require primary sources or qualified people.
- **Incoming transition:** Same-model opposition exposes gaps but cannot supply the
  external fact needed to resolve the real decision.
- **Outgoing transition or mini-hook:** Close with the declarative rule: a second opinion
  is an independent route to a conclusion that reality can still overturn.
- **Promise or loop payoff:** Pays the four-question promise, title question,
  possible-answer/safe-decision distinction, viewer-immunity defense, and
  `SECOND OPINION?` motif.
